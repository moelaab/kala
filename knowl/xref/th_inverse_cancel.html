Theorem 2.3.3. Solving with invertible matrices.

Suppose \(A\) is an invertible \(n\times n\) matrix. Then
\begin{equation*} AX=Y \iff X=A^{-1}Y \end{equation*}
for any \(n\times r\) matrices \(X\) and \(Y\text{,}\) and
\begin{equation*} ZA=W \iff Z=WA^{-1} \end{equation*}
for any \(r\times n\) matrices \(Z\) and \(W\text{.}\)
In other words, matrix equations of this sort have unique solutions obtained by multiplying on the left or right by \(A^{-1}\text{.}\)

Proof.

We give a proof only for the the first equation: \(AX=Y\text{.}\) To show the equivalence we must show the two implications \(AX=Y\implies X=A^{-1}Y\) and \(X=A^{-1}Y\implies AX=Y\text{.}\) To this end, we have
\begin{align*} AX=Y \amp\implies A^{-1}AX=A^{-1}Y \\ \amp\implies IX=A^{-1}Y \\ \amp\implies X=A^{-1}Y \text{,} \end{align*}
and
\begin{align*} X=A^{-1}Y \amp\implies AX=AA^{-1}Y \\ \amp\implies AX=IY \\ \amp\implies AX=Y \text{.} \end{align*}
