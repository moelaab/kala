Theorem 1.5.26. Determinant is multiplicative.

Let \(A\) and \(B\) be \(n\times n\) matrices. Then
\begin{equation*} \det AB=\det A\, \det B\text{.} \end{equation*}

Proof.

We consider two cases based on the invertibility of \(A\) and/or \(B\text{.}\)
\(A\) or \(B\) is not invertible.
In this case \(AB\) is not invertible (Corollary 1.4.14), and hence \(\det AB=0\) by Theorem 1.5.25. By the same reasoning we must have \(\det A=0\) or \(\det B=0\text{.}\) It follows that
\begin{equation*} \det AB=\det A\, \det B=0 \end{equation*}
in this case.
\(A\) and \(B\) both invertible.
In this case we can write
\begin{align*} A \amp E_1E_2\cdots E_r \amp B\amp=E'_1E'_2\cdots E'_s \end{align*}
for some elementary matrices \(E_i\) and \(E'_j\) (Theorem 1.4.5). Then
\begin{align*} \det AB \amp= \det(E_1E_2\cdots E_rE'_1E'_2\cdots E'_s) \\ \amp= \det E_1\det E_2\cdots \det E_r\det E'_1\det E'_2\cdots \det E'_s \amp (\knowl{./knowl/xref/cor_det_prod_elem.html}{\text{Corollary 1.5.23}})\\ \amp=\det A\, \det B \amp (\knowl{./knowl/xref/cor_det_prod_elem.html}{\text{Corollary 1.5.23}}) \text{.} \end{align*}
